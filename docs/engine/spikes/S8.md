# S8: cross-process GPU contention

Spike S8 from plan section 26, Phase 0. Lane: Engine and platform. It informs
contract 13 (GPU time slicing and deadline signals, RFC 13) and gates Phase 2.

**Pass criteria (plan, section 25 S8 row).** 4K playback in the interactive engine
while the job engine exports 4K blur and Blender EEVEE renders; zero drops over 60 s.

**Result: with frame-aligned slicing, no `gate-pause` run (six 60 s runs in five
sessions) had a late frame attributed to the job engine's command buffers, and five of
the six dropped nothing. Four qualifications apply.** (1) The row as written is
stricter than A alone can meet. (2) "Not caused by the job engine" is only partly measurable: the
attribution sees the job engine's own command buffers, not its encoder or Blender.
(3) The 2 ms bound held only at a 1 ms slice target once other GPU clients were active.
(4) A variant with a heavier interactive frame failed on the active desktop, where that
heavier frame alone was already over budget much of the time.

With the recommended policy (job-engine work cut into command buffers and dispatches
of at most 2 ms, one in flight, each submitted only into the gap after the interactive
frame completes; utility QoS; a 100 ms yield after a miss; Blender paused for
playback), 4K playback at 60 fps next to a 4K blur export, a flat-out HEVC Main10
encoder session, and Blender EEVEE (`gate-pause`):

|---|---|---|---|
| `run3` | active session, but 8% / 3% external load around this row (on) | 0 / 0 / 0 | 0 |
| `run4` | quiet (on, screen locked) | 1 / 0 / 0 | 0 (the late frame is `other`) |

`run3` used the build before the fix that holds slices until the interactive frame's
phase is known; its on-screen and paced-encoder variants each had 1–2 late frames
attributed to B, at the start of playback (section 5). With the heavier interactive
frame (`aheavy-gate-pause`: 8 layers, radius-40 blur, about 10 ms of GPU per frame)
the row dropped 1,167 / 55 / 7 on the active desktop (`run3`), 0 on the quiet desktop
(`run4`), and 2 / 0 / 0 under a one-client stand-in (`run8`); under the three-client
stand-in that frame alone exceeded the GPU (section 5).

Without slicing the same export dropped 1,068 of 3,600 frames under the busy-desktop
stand-in, and single long dispatches dropped every frame. Reactive slicing (2 ms
command buffers and yields, but not aligned to the interactive frame) failed under the
stand-in with the display on (`run6`: 213 to 3,371 drops across its variants), but by
very different amounts elsewhere (`sliced-2ms` dropped 283 there and 1 in `run7`). Background QoS and the Darwin background state did not help; the private
Metal queue priority did. A alone misses the 1-frame deadline about once a minute on
this machine (27 frames in 77 paired 20 s controls), so "zero drops over 60 s" holds
for the job engine's share, not unconditionally. Contract 13 needs one new signal
(per-frame completion) for the alignment; see
[Recommended contract 13 parameters](#recommended-contract-13-parameters).

Reference machine only (decision 11): Apple M4 Max MacBook Pro, 128 GB, built-in XDR
display at 120 Hz, macOS 27.0 (26A428), Rust 1.96.1 (`engine/env.sh`), wgpu 30.0.1
(Metal), Blender 4.5.14 LTS. Base-machine results are **unverified**.

## What was built

Two processes, each with its own wgpu Metal device and command queue, plus an
orchestrator. All three share the `mach_absolute_time` host clock, which is also the
domain of Metal GPU timestamps on Apple silicon, so CPU and GPU times from both
processes compare directly.

- **Process A, `s8-interactive`** (the interactive engine). Every frame composites 3
  `rgba16float` 4K layers, runs a separable blur over the 4K result, and writes an
  8-bit output into a ring of 8 surfaces (or into a `CAMetalLayer` drawable). Frames
  are paced at 60 fps by a real-time (Mach time-constraint) thread with
  `mach_wait_until` on an absolute vsync grid: one display-link vsync anchors the grid
  and it advances by the frame period 1/fps, where fps is derived from the nominal
  refresh as 1 / (k × refresh), k = 2 on the 120 Hz panel (fps = 60.0003 here). A completion
  thread waits on each submission with a deadline, so A signals `AtRisk` (not done at
  75% of the period) and `Miss` (not done at the deadline) the moment they happen,
  not after completion, plus `FrameOk` or `LateDone` with the frame's tick. Per frame
  it records CPU submit, GPU begin and end (pass timestamps resolved in batches
  outside the measured command buffers), and completion.
- **Process B, `s8-job`** (the job engine). An export-like compute workload: per
  export frame, a procedural 4K source pass and 6 separable Gaussian blur iterations
  (radius 48) between two 4K `rgba16float` targets. Modes:
  - `full`: one export frame (13 dependent dispatches) per command buffer, 2 in
    flight, default QoS. How a naive exporter submits.
  - `heavy`: one long dispatch per command buffer, 2 in flight; the radius sets the
    dispatch length (a foreign renderer or one heavy effect).
  - `sliced` (contract 13): work is cut into horizontal bands and packed into command
    buffers whose predicted GPU time stays under `slice-ms`. Per-row costs are
    calibrated from GPU timestamps before the run and corrected continuously from
    measured command-buffer times (EMA of measured / predicted). Options: command
    buffers in flight, thread QoS, Darwin background state, yield on miss or on
    at-risk for `yield-ms`, an emulated relay delay, and **frame alignment**: submit a
    slice only once A's frame for the current period is done and only if the slice is
    predicted to finish 1 ms before A's next tick.
  - `none`: no GPU work (encoder or Blender only).
- **VideoToolbox sessions** in B (`vt.rs`): 1 to 4 concurrent hardware compression
  sessions (HEVC Main10 from `x420`, H.264 High from `420v`, ProRes 422 HQ from
  `x422`), 4K, fed from IOSurface-backed pool buffers with moving content, at most 16
  frames pending, flat out or paced (`--vt-fps`).
- **Blender worker** (`blender.rs`, `blender/eevee_load.py`). B launches Blender the
  way Powermove's worker does (`--background --factory-startup --disable-autoexec`)
  and renders the plan's 3D export gate scene in a loop: 3 subdivided models with
  principled materials, sun and area lights with soft shadows, EEVEE, 16 samples,
  3840×2160, AgX, transparent film, 16-bit RGBA PNG (decision 15). Objects move every
  frame. B also stands in for main's Blender supervision and applies a playback
  policy from the same deadline signals: `run`, `bg` (Darwin background state),
  `pause` (`SIGSTOP` on `PlaybackStart`, `SIGCONT` on stop), `yield` (`SIGSTOP` for
  the yield window after a miss), `throttle` (10 ms running, 40 ms stopped while
  playing).
- **Signals.** A writes fixed 24-byte records (kind, send time, frame tick) into a
  FIFO that B drains between submissions, standing in for main's relay. `--relay-delay-ms`
  holds every record in B until that long after it was sent.
- **Orchestrator, `s8-run`.** Per scenario: wait for a quiet GPU, run the **paired
  control** (A alone, same A arguments, 20 s), start B and wait for its calibration
  (and Blender's first two frames), run A for 60 s, stop B, and sample the GPU for 3 s
  after. A scenario is rerun once when its control dropped frames or the GPU was busy
  before or after. It records display state, IORegistry GPU utilization, and both
  processes' summaries, and **attributes every late frame** by overlapping A's
  per-frame GPU spans with B's command-buffer GPU spans and yield windows:
  - `b-start`: A's GPU start was delayed by more than 1 ms and B's command buffers
    covered at least half of the delay.
  - `b-concurrent`: A started promptly but B's command buffers ran during at least
    half of A's execution and slowed it.
  - `backlog`: A's previous frame was still on the GPU at submit. This class takes
    precedence, so a frame queued behind a B-caused late frame is `backlog`, and
    `b-start + b-concurrent` is a **lower bound** on late frames caused by B's command
    buffers. Each backlog queue is therefore also traced to the class of the late frame
    that started it (`late_backlog_after_b` in the JSON); in the aligned and gate runs it
    adds at most 1 frame (`run3 aheavy-gate-pause`).
  - `other`: B's command buffers were mostly idle. The cause is the encoder, Blender,
    WindowServer, other apps, or A's own CPU side.

  **The attribution cannot see the encoder's or Blender's GPU work**: only B's own
  command buffers carry timestamps. Both are part of the job engine's workload, so an
  `other` frame is "not caused by the job engine's command buffers", not "not caused by
  the job engine". In the gate scenarios Blender is stopped from the first frame of
  playback; the encoder is not, and its share is bounded only indirectly by the
  encoder-only scenarios (section 3) and by the paired controls.
- **Presentation (`--present`).** A 16×9 pt borderless, click-through, non-activating
  window (accessory activation policy, never key) in the bottom-left corner hosts a
  `CAMetalLayer` with a 3840×2160 drawable. A renders its last pass into the drawable,
  presents it with a Metal command buffer on the same queue, and records each
  drawable's `presentedTime`. An offscreen window gets no presented times, so the
  corner window is the smallest one the compositor actually shows.

### Measurements

- **Drops at 1, 2, and 3 frames.** A frame is dropped at the 1-frame deadline when
  its GPU work completes more than one frame period after its tick (no render-ahead:
  the gesture and scrub path, and the strictest playback model), at the 2- and 3-frame
  deadlines with that much render-ahead (section 15's adaptive ring). Frames never
  submitted (ring full, or the pacing thread woke after the next tick) count as drops
  at every deadline. The S8 row is judged at the 1-frame deadline.
- **Latency**: tick to GPU completion. **Jitter**: |completion interval − period|.
  **GPU start delay**: CPU submit to the first GPU timestamp of the frame.
- **On glass** (`present-*` only): `presentedTime` per frame; an *irregular interval*
  is a pair of consecutive presented frames whose spacing differs from the frame
  period by more than half a refresh (a repeat or a skip).

## Conditions

Every scenario ran with A for 60 s (3,600 frames at 60 fps) on the reference machine
while it was in normal use. What else used the GPU mattered more than anything the
spike controlled, so results are grouped by desktop state:

- **Active desktop** (`run3`): the machine in interactive use with foreground apps
  (including video playback) and other GPU work running; 8–55% of GPU time used by
  other processes in the 3 s windows around most scenarios. The orchestrator waits for a
  window under 10% before each attempt, so a row's load varies: some `run3` rows,
  including `gate-pause` (8%/3%), ran in a lull. The desktop went quiet during the last
  third of the run (from `sliced-2ms-120` on, 2–8%); the `ext %` column shows each
  row's load, and "active" describes the session, not every row.
- **Quiet desktop** (`run4` and `run5`): nobody at the machine, 0–11% external GPU
  time; for part of `run4` the screen was locked (recorded per scenario).
- **Busy-desktop stand-in** (`run6`, `run7`): the quiet machine plus three unmeasured
  `s8-interactive` processes (`--desk="--fps 60 --layers 2 --blur-radius 8" --desk-n 3`),
  each with its own device rendering 4K frames at 60 fps, about 50% GPU time in total.
  This reproduces the active-desktop behaviour on demand (heavy: 1,800 of 1,800 frames
  dropped in a 30 s probe, versus 0 on the quiet desktop) without depending on how the
  machine happens to be used. `run8` used one such process (`--desk-n 1`) for the
  heavier interactive frame, which three of them overload. The display state differed
  between and within these runs (the `disp` column): in `run6` the slicing scenarios
  ran with the display on and the Blender and gate scenarios with it asleep; `run7`
  and `run8` ran with it asleep, so WindowServer was idle. Reactive slicing in
  particular dropped far less with the display asleep (section 2).

The paired control (A alone with the same desktop load, 20 s before each scenario)
shows the noise floor. Sample: runs 3 to 5, the default A at 60 fps only (the 120 fps
and `aheavy` scenarios are excluded, since their A is a different workload), and
attempts whose before/after external load stayed under 60% (this drops 2 kept
controls, `run3 sliced-2ms-noyield` and `full-bg`, and their attempts). In that sample
77 control runs of 20 s (26 minutes in all) dropped 27 frames at the 1-frame deadline,
about one a minute, in 16 of the 77; the 67 controls kept with their scenarios dropped
6 at the 1-frame deadline and none at the 2-frame deadline. Under the stand-in the 22
kept controls dropped none. The excluded A workloads are not near that floor: the
`aheavy` controls in `run3` dropped 0, 332, 269, 209, and 0 frames in 20 s (and its
60 s `aheavy-baseline` 595), and the 120 fps controls 156, 73, 1, and 0. A alone misses the 1-frame deadline occasionally even on
an idle GPU: its frame takes 6–7 ms at the clocks an idle GPU runs at, and rare frames
reach 17–23 ms. With B running, the GPU stays at higher clocks and A's frame takes 2–3
ms (sliced-2ms-align p50 2.8 ms versus baseline 6.7 ms in the same run).

Table columns: `ctl` control drops (1-frame); drops at the 1-, 2-, and 3-frame
deadlines; `skip` frames never submitted (included in the drops); A latency p50, p99,
max (ms); completion jitter p99; A's GPU execution p50 and p99; A's GPU start delay p99;
B's export frames per second; B's command-buffer GPU span p99 (ms); encoder fps per
session; Blender frames finished; late-frame attribution (`b-start`/`b-concurrent`/
`backlog`/`other`); `b after B`, the backlog frames whose queue started at a `b-start`
or `b-concurrent` frame; on-glass irregular intervals; external GPU % before/after;
`disp`, the display state (`on`, `locked`: on with the screen locked, `off`: asleep,
so WindowServer was idle).

- **Reactive slicing** (≤ 2 ms command buffers, 1 in flight, utility QoS, 100 ms
  yield after a miss) is not enough, and how badly it fails varies a lot from run to
  run. It held 0 drops on the quiet desktop. Under the stand-in with the display on
  (`run6`), `sliced-2ms` dropped 283 frames at the 1-frame deadline (0 at 2 frames),
  with background QoS 213, with 2 in flight 417, and without the yield 3,371. The same
  `sliced-2ms` under the stand-in with the display asleep (`run7`) dropped 1, and an
  independent 30 s rerun (display asleep) 5 of 1,800. Treat 213 to 3,371 as one
  session's magnitudes, not stable figures; what repeats is the direction (reactive
  runs had late frames attributed to B, aligned runs in the same sessions had none). On
  the active desktop `sliced-1ms` dropped 40 and `sliced-4ms` 11. Under the stand-in
  the late frames were almost all `b-concurrent`: A started on time but ran alongside a
  slice and stretched past the deadline (A's GPU time p99 19.5 ms versus 8.5 ms aligned
  in `run6`).
- **Frame-aligned slicing removes the overlap.** B submits a slice only after A's
  frame for the current period is done, and only if the slice is predicted to end 1 ms
  before A's next tick. With the build used for runs 4 to 8, no aligned run at 60 fps
  had a late frame attributed to B's command buffers (`b-start`, `b-concurrent`, or a
  backlog behind one), on the quiet desktop or under the stand-in: `sliced-1ms-align`,
  `sliced-2ms-align`, `sliced-4ms-align`, `heavy-sliced` (the radius-384 kernel cut into
  bands), the relay variants, and every `gate-pause` and `aheavy` aligned run. `run3`
  (active desktop, the build before the playback-start fix) had five aligned runs with
  1–2 such frames each: `gate-pause-vt30` 1 and `present-gate-pause` 2 at the start of
  playback (what the fix addresses), `sliced-2ms-align-relay1` 1 `b-start`,
  `aheavy-sliced-2ms-align` 1 `b-start` (B covered 0.9 ms of a 1.3 ms start delay; the

  frame queued behind them, out of 1,167 late frames. A's latency also falls (p50

  up. The other late frames in aligned runs came while B's command buffers were idle:
  `export-align+hevc10` in `run3` (26, all `backlog`/`other`), and the `aheavy` runs
  below.

- **Throughput cost.** Aligned slicing ran the export at 6.2 fps versus 12.3 unsliced

- **Yield after a miss.** With alignment B almost never yielded (0 or 1 yield in each

  reactive slicing it matters: without it the stand-in run dropped 3,371 frames instead

### 7. Clock

The first runs paced A on every k-th `CVDisplayLink` callback. Under GPU load the
callbacks on this machine arrive at about 114 Hz instead of 120 Hz with 5–14 ms gaps
(`examples/vsync_probe.rs`: on an idle machine the `output` host times are exact to
the microsecond and the `now` times jitter ±1 ms; under load both stray), and the
refresh measured from them was off by up to 4%. Those runs (`run1`, `run2`) were
discarded. A now paces on an absolute vsync grid anchored on one display-link
`output` time and advanced by k nominal refreshes per frame. The interactive engine should do
the same (or use `CAMetalDisplayLink` on its view, not measured here) rather than
schedule frames from raw `CVDisplayLink` callbacks.

## Recommended contract 13 parameters

Changes to RFC 13 (`pm_protocol::slicing`, draft version 0):

1. **Keep `max_command_buffer_us = 2_000`, bound dispatches too, and predict to half
   the bound.** No single dispatch may run longer than the slice: long passes are cut
   into bands (rows or tiles), as `heavy-sliced` does. Slices are predicted from
   per-pass, per-row costs calibrated with GPU timestamps and corrected continuously
   from measured command-buffer times. Filling to 90% of 2 ms does not hold the bound
   when other GPU clients are active: under the stand-in, command buffers ran p99
   3.15–3.27 ms and max 3.81–4.05 ms during playback, with 12–15% over 2.5 ms (p99
   2.05–2.74 ms on the quiet desktop and in the active session's lulls). A 1 ms target
   kept every command buffer under the stand-in at 2.01 ms or less (p99 1.43–1.52 ms)
   with 0 drops in `gate-pause-1ms`, at a throughput cost of 0–37% (8% in the gate
   scenario). Either use a 1 ms target, or correct the cost model from a high
   percentile of measured / predicted time instead of its mean (not measured here).
   With alignment the overshoot caused no drops at 60 fps; the bound matters at 120 fps.
2. **Add `max_in_flight = 1` during playback.** With reactive slicing two in flight
   dropped 417 frames under the stand-in versus 283 with one, and 30 versus 0 on the
   active desktop. Alignment implies one in flight.
3. **Add frame alignment; this is the mechanism that held the S8 row.** The job
   engine submits a slice only after the interactive engine's frame for the current
   period is done, and only if the slice is predicted to end `align_guard_us = 1_000`
   before the next frame tick. At playback start it submits nothing until it knows the
   phase (two finished frames). This needs a per-frame signal, so `DeadlineSignal` gains
   `FrameDone { at, frame_seq, tick, late }` (or `PlaybackStarted` carries the vsync
   anchor and period and `FrameDone` is sent per frame). Adding a signal is a version
   bump under the RFC's own rules; it should happen before the Phase 2 freeze.
4. **Deliver `Deadline` events on a direct port** between the engines (the RFC's
   fallback): with a signal per frame, 4 ms of relay latency cut export throughput by
   24–55%; 1 ms cost nothing measurable.
5. **Keep `yield_after_miss_us = 100_000`, fixed.** With alignment it almost never
   fires; scaling it by `overrun_us` (open question 1) is not worth the complexity.
   Yield on miss, not on at-risk.
6. **QoS utility for the job engine; never the Darwin background state.** Neither has
   a measurable GPU effect (the RFC's question); the Darwin background state slows the job
   engine's reaction to signals.
7. **Blender: `Pause` during playback, and drop `Throttle { max_workers }` for
   playback.** A worker count does not bound GPU bursts, and a `SIGSTOP` duty cycle
   leaked bursts (18 and 96 drops). Open question 3 is answered: finer `SIGSTOP` throttling
   does not work.
8. **Hardware encode sessions** (contract 14 arbitration): sessions share a fixed
   per-codec engine throughput (HEVC about 120 fps at 4K, ProRes 422 HQ about 1,145),
   so main should cap concurrent sessions per codec and queue the rest. Encoders did
   not measurably contend with playback; pacing them is optional.
9. **Agent snapshots** (open question 2): aligned 4 ms slices also held 0 drops at 60
   fps, so agent snapshots could use 4 ms slices at 60 fps for latency, but not at 120
   fps, where only the shorter slices fit. One 2 ms bound (1 ms target) for the whole
   job engine is the safe default; a per-class slice is possible later without a wire
   change.
10. **The interactive engine pre-rolls** at least two frames before
    `PlaybackStarted`, and keeps two frames of render-ahead (section 15): every
    `gate-pause` run was drop-free at the 2-frame deadline, while A alone misses the
    1-frame deadline about once a minute.
11. **Private `setGPUPriority:`** is the only OS-level lever that worked: the job
    engine's queue at priority 2 removed every drop from unsliced work under the
    stand-in (`full` 1,068 → 0, `heavy` 3,600 → 0) while keeping 65–80% of its
    throughput. It is private API (a selector on Apple's `IOGPUMetalCommandQueue`,
    values 0 to 2; others abort), it covers only queues we own (not Blender), and it can
    change in any macOS release. Using it as defence in depth behind a capability check
    is a product decision for Jude; the spike does not adopt it. Aligned slicing alone
    met the row with the default interactive frame; it was not enough for the heavier
    frame on the active desktop (section 2), where priority 2 was not tried.

## Gaps and caveats

- **Base machines are unverified** (decision 11). The M4 Max has far more GPU than a
  base M1 Air; the policy's margins there are unknown.
- **The S8 row as written cannot be met by A alone** at the 1-frame deadline on this
  machine (about one miss per minute with nothing else running). A workable restatement:
  zero drops at the 2-frame deadline (section 15's render-ahead), and at the 1-frame
  deadline zero late frames overlapping the job engine's GPU work and no more drops
  than A's paired control rate, under the busy-desktop stand-in with the display on.
  The overlap part depends on an attribution that sees only the job engine's own
  command buffers, not its hardware encoder or Blender (Blender is stopped for
  playback; the encoder is not); the paired-control part is what covers the encoder.
  That is a plan change for Jude; this report does not edit the plan.
- **Attribution limits.** `other` means B's command buffers were idle, not that the
  job engine was; `b-start + b-concurrent` is a lower bound because `backlog` takes
  precedence (the `b after B` column gives the queues behind B-caused frames). The
  noise-floor sample excludes the 120 fps and `aheavy` workloads and attempts above 60%
  external load (Conditions).
- **The heavier interactive frame failed the row on the active desktop** (`run3`,
  1,167 drops) and was not rerun there; the cause could not be separated between the
  desktop load (the same A alone dropped 595) and the encoder. It held on the quiet
  desktop and dropped 2 under the one-client stand-in.
- **A and B are stand-ins**: A is a 3-layer composite with blur, not the real engine;
  B's export is blur passes, and the encoder is fed from a fixed pool of pre-filled
  buffers rather than B's rendered frames (no GPU colour conversion pass before encode,
  which in the product is sliced like any other pass).
- **The busy-desktop stand-in** is three synthetic Metal clients. It reproduces the
  active-desktop failure of unsliced work, but it is not WindowServer, a browser's
  video decode, or Electron; the active-desktop runs (`run3`) are the real thing and
  could not be repeated on demand. Part of `run6`, including its `gate-pause`, and all of
  `run7` and `run8` ran with the display asleep and the screen locked, so WindowServer
  was idle: no `gate-pause` run combines the stand-in with the display on.
  `present-gate-pause` was skipped for the same reason, and on-glass results exist only
  for `run3` (active) and `run5` (quiet). Reactive slicing dropped 283 frames under the
  stand-in with the display on and 1 with it asleep, so the display state matters.
- **External load** is read from the AGX user clients' `accumulatedGPUTime` in the
  IORegistry, only in the 3 s windows before and after a scenario: while the spike's
  own processes run, those counters charged A's GPU time to unrelated processes, so a
  during-run reading is not evidence. The paired control is the during-run check.
- **Presentation** was measured through a 16×9 pt window; the real viewer window and
  the S1 bridge were not part of S8.
- **Why** the GPU stops sharing when more clients are active is not visible from user
  space; the report states what was measured, not the scheduler's design.
- **`CAMetalDisplayLink`** was not tried as A's clock.
- **120 fps** was measured only for reactive and aligned 2 ms slicing (`run3`): A alone
  at 120 fps already dropped 73 frames in its control, and aligned slicing dropped 24
  (all `other`).
