# S7: 3D

Phase 0 spike S7 (plan sections 7, 8, 9, 17, 25; appendix A items 38 to 40; Phase 0
table). Questions: can wgpu reproduce three.js r180's 3D output within the section 2 3D
bar, preferably by compiling three.js's own GLSL through the S3 pipeline, and how does
hand-written WGSL compare? Can `pm-geom3d` in wasm meet the 1M-vertex skinned pick
targets with today's pick semantics? Does Blender with the section 17 worker settings
give 16-bit straight-alpha AgX surfaces, and may a frame's layers be batched in one
invocation? Are appendix A items 38 and 39 real?

Code: `engine/spikes/s7-3d/` (own Cargo workspace, Rust 1.96.1; bun scripts). How to run
everything: its `README.md`. Measured on one machine (decision 11): Apple M4 Max, 128 GB,
macOS 27. three.js 0.180.0 (`apps/desktop/node_modules`), Chromium 151.0.7922.34
headless with ANGLE on Metal, wgpu 30.0.1 (Metal), naga 30.0.1, glslang 0.8.1, Node
24.18.0 (V8 13.6.233.17), bun 1.3.14, Blender 4.5.14 LTS (OCIO 2.4.1). Other workers
shared the machine (load average 5 to 10), so timings are upper bounds. Dates:
2026-10-11 and 12.

## Answer

- **3D bar: 80 of 80 outputs pass** (15 scenes, 18.4 million pixels) with Metal's safe
  math mode; **79 of 80** with wgpu's default (fast math). 99.998% of all pixels are
  within 2 CV (bar: 99.5% per output; worst output 99.930%), the largest difference
  outside MSAA edges and penumbrae is 10 CV (bar: 12), and the smallest per-object SSIM
  is 0.9992 (bar: 0.99). With fast math one transparent box moves its MSAA coverage by a
  sample along one edge: 4 pixels at 38 CV where the reference's MSAA and non-MSAA
  renders agree, so they are not classified as edge pixels. The default path, three.js's generated GLSL through the S3 pipeline, needed
  nine pipeline fixes, all done here (see "Pipeline findings").
- **Hand-written WGSL** for the standard material (directional lights with PCF-soft
  shadows, hemisphere and ambient light, the physical BRDF, ACES Filmic, sRGB output)
  replaced 12 of the 75 program instances in the corpus and produced **byte-identical
  outputs** to the compiled GLSL in all 80 outputs, in both math modes. It compiles in
  1.1 ms against 25 ms (median) for the GLSL path, but every r180 chunk combination would
  have to be re-derived and maintained by hand.
- **Picks (wasm in V8): pass.** On the 1M-vertex skinned model, the first pick after a
  time change takes 0.010 ms median, 0.075 ms p95, 0.19 ms max over 200 time changes
  (target 33 ms); picks at an unchanged frame take 0.013 ms median, 0.24 ms max over
  2,000 rays inside the model's footprint (target 2 ms). Every pick equals three.js's
  raycast (11,088 of 11,088 rays across 4 scenes and 10 times: same layer, same kind,
  distance within 6.4e-8 relative). Today's production pick on the same model takes 115
  to 229 ms per hit. Skinned picks are lazy: BVH nodes are bounded from per-joint rest
  boxes and only visited leaves are skinned. Re-skinning all vertices and refitting
  instead costs 13.0 ms median in wasm (8.3 ms native), also within target.
- **Blender (4.5.14 LTS) with the section 17 settings: pass.** Surfaces are 16-bit RGBA
  PNG with straight alpha, and their codes are AgX: Cycles matches Blender's OCIO AgX
  processor exactly at 16 bits on 90 emission patches at alpha 1, 0.5, and 0.25; EEVEE
  is within 8 of 65,535 (half-float render buffers); alpha is exact in both. A
  premultiply-then-transform hypothesis misses by up to 27,969 codes.
- **Batching equality: pass** where the renderer is deterministic. Rendering a frame's
  layers in one invocation gives pixel-identical surfaces to one fresh process per layer
  with the pinned worker for EEVEE (6 scenes, 23 layers) and Cycles on the CPU (4
  scenes, 13 layers), and with today's 8-bit worker (EEVEE, 4 scenes, 13 layers). Cycles on the Metal GPU is not deterministic
  run to run (±1 of 65,535 on 4 to 114 channel values per layer), with or without
  batching. With PNG metadata stamps off, files are byte-identical too.
- **Appendix A item 38 confirmed:** Blender's orthographic renders are 1.78 times larger
  than native on a 16:9 frame (every object, both axes, both engines): Blender fits
  `ortho_scale` (the native full height, 6 / zoom) to the frame width.
- **Appendix A item 39 confirmed:** glTF source textures render vertically flipped in
  Blender (EEVEE and Cycles, today's worker and the pinned one).
- **Gate status:** S7's pass criteria are met with three conditions. (1) 80 of 80
  requires Metal's safe math mode, which wgpu 30 does not expose; the spike patches
  wgpu-hal. (2) The wgpu side replays the WebGL2 command stream three.js issued; it
  proves translation and GL-state semantics on wgpu, not the engine generating those
  draws and programs itself (the port the recommendation describes). (3) AVIF textures
  cannot be decoded inside the wasm build (rav1d does not compile for
  wasm32-unknown-unknown); geometry queries do not need texels, so the browser decodes
  them there.

### Pipeline findings

Each was found by a failing output, fixed in `s7-glsl` or the replayer, and covered by a
test or the corpus. The engine's shader pipeline (S3) and 3D pass need all of them:

1. **Varyings must be linked by name.** glslang numbers each stage's varyings in
   declaration order; r180's transmission program declares `vWorldPosition` and `vNormal`
   in different orders per stage, so the fragment stage read the normal as the world
   position (transmission SSIM 0.36). Fixed with program-wide explicit locations.
2. **Write an all-ones sample mask from every fragment shader.** Without it, Apple GPUs
   shade 2×2 quads that span two triangles of one draw, so `dFdx`/`dFdy` mix
   primitives at every triangle edge: flat-shaded normals and `geometryRoughness` go
   wrong (flat-shaded sphere SSIM 0.955). ANGLE's Metal back end always writes the mask
   (its `writeHelperSampleMask` path, seen in the translated MSL). naga's SPIR-V front
   end rejects GLSL's `gl_SampleMask`, so the mask is added on the naga IR. This fix also
   took 2 CV agreement from 99.62% to 100.000% on the extension grid.
3. **Metal math mode.** wgpu compiles MSL with Metal's default fast math. Safe math fixes
   the one failing output; relaxed math does too; neither changes anything else beyond
   noise (`gltf-pbr-ext` goes from 1 to 8 CV max, still passing). wgpu 30 has no API
   for `MTLCompileOptions.mathMode`; `patches/` carries the 7-line wgpu-hal change.
4. **std140 scalar and vec2 uniform arrays** (`float a[N]`, 16-byte stride) are laid out
   at the element size by naga's MSL back end, moving later members; widened to vec4
   arrays with swizzled access.
5. **std140 struct spans:** SPIR-V does not carry struct sizes, so a struct in an array
   got its natural span instead of the array stride; spans are padded.
6. **MSL vertex pulling bound:** naga's vertex pulling admits element i only when
   i < bound size / stride, so an attribute at an offset inside an interleaved buffer
   (`instanceMatrix` columns) loses its last element; buffers are padded by 256 bytes.
7. **`modf`:** naga's SPIR-V front end produces an unregistered result type; rewritten as
   `x - trunc(x)` (exact).
8. **Arrays of varyings and matrix attributes** become one varying or attribute per
   element or column (naga accepts neither in entry-point IO).
9. **Vulkan GLSL keywords:** three.js's transmission code names a parameter `sampler`.

Translation cost: `s7_glsl::compile` takes 25 ms median, 38 ms p95, 97 ms max per
program (1.2 s for all 49); naga's MSL back end adds 0.4 ms; Metal's own compile is not
included. The engine needs a persistent program cache keyed by source.

## pm-geom3d prototype

`crates/s7-geom3d` builds natively and for wasm32-unknown-unknown (1.13 MB stripped,
343 KB gzipped) with a small C ABI that V8 drives directly (`bench/picks.mjs`).

- **glTF decode:** the `gltf` crate parses the document and GLB container; accessors are
  read here (normalized integers for KHR_mesh_quantization, sparse accessors, strides,
  matrix column padding); extensions the crate does not model come from the raw JSON
  (EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing, EXT_texture_webp and EXT_texture_avif sources, required
  extension checks). Strips and fans convert as three.js does.
- **Images:** PNG and JPEG (pure Rust), WebP (`image-webp`, also in wasm): identical to
  the texels Chromium handed WebGL (0 of 262,144 values differ). AVIF (`avif-parse` +
  `rav1d` through its dav1d API, native only): within 1 CV of Chromium (3,822 of 262,144
  values differ by 1, from Y′CbCr rounding). rav1d 1.1.0 and re_rav1d do not compile for
  wasm32-unknown-unknown (`libc` has no `ptrdiff_t` there) and avif-decode 2 and 3
  require rustc 1.98.
- **Animation** follows three.js r180: every clip at weight 1, looping, sampled with
  `setTime`; linear, step, and glTF cubic-spline interpolants (quaternions through
  `slerpFlat`, splines normalized); clips sharing a property blended in clip order by
  the `PropertyMixer` rule. Bone world matrices match three.js within 6.7e-16 at every
  tested time, morph weights exactly.
- **Skinning and BVH:** linear blend skinning in f32 from f64 joint matrices; binned SAH
  BVH (leaves of 4) over the rest pose; refit bottom-up; or the lazy path (below).
- **Pick semantics** are three.js's: `Raycaster.setFromCamera`, `Ray.intersectTriangle`
  with single-sided culling, `InstancedMesh` per instance, `Points` and `Line` with
  threshold 1 divided by the object's local scale and GLTFLoader's accessor-bounds
  sphere pre-test, closest hit first, meshes whose materials all have opacity ≤ 0
  skipped, locked and hidden layers skipped, restricted to a depth set.

The lazy path bounds a BVH node, for the current pose, by the union over the joints
that influence its vertices of the node's rest box moved by each joint matrix (a
skinned vertex with non-negative weights summing to 1 lies in the convex hull of its
per-joint images), padded for f32 rounding; leaves the ray reaches skin only their
vertices, with the same arithmetic as the full path, so hits are identical. Primitives
with active morph targets or unnormalized weights use the full path. Earlier runs of
the full path under heavier machine load measured 20 to 59 ms for the first pick, so
the full path alone would not hold the target reliably on a loaded base machine; the
lazy path is the recommended design. Load and memory are not optimized (accessors go
through f64 vectors; steady state is about 115 MB).

The prototype's posed geometry also matches a Blender snapshot's (`renderSnapshot`
positions × `matrixWorld`): 3.1e-7 maximum on the skinned and morphing model, 9.1e-9 on
quantized geometry, 4.4e-16 on rigid meshes, against the section 25 bound of 1e-6.

## Blender

Blender 4.5.14 LTS, official arm64 build (SHA-256 checked), installed in
`/Applications/Blender.app`. `blender/run.ts` runs the shipped worker
(`apps/desktop/src/main/blender-worker.ts`) with `BlenderWorker`'s flags, request
files, and stdin envelopes; snapshots come from a port of `renderSnapshot` over the
production runtime. Variants: `today` (the worker unchanged: 8-bit PNG, default dither)
and `pinned` (section 17: `color_depth '16'`, `view_transform 'AgX'`, `look 'None'`,
`dither_intensity 0`, display `sRGB`, gamma 1, image color management following the
scene, compression 15), optionally with metadata stamps off.

**Surface format.** Pinned surfaces are 16-bit RGBA PNG. Chunks: `IHDR`, `eXIf`,
`oFFs`, `pHYs`, text metadata, `IDAT`; no `gAMA`, `sRGB`, or `iCCP` (the import ignores
them anyway). `s7-blender` decodes codes exactly (`code / 65535`) and premultiplies in E
at f32, the compositor's input; its test feeds a PNG with a `gAMA` chunk. In this
corpus the pinned 16-bit surfaces are smaller than today's dithered 8-bit ones (15 to 70
KB against 55 to 97 KB per layer).

**AgX and alpha** (`blender/agx_check.py`): emission patches at 10 scene-linear values
(0 to 16) in 3 tints, each at alpha 1, 0.5, and 0.25, on a transparent film, compared
with Blender's own OCIO processor (Linear Rec.709 → sRGB display, AgX view):

| Engine | Max code difference vs AgX(E) straight | vs AgX(a·E) / a | Alpha |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cycles (CPU, 16 samples) | 0 | 27,962 | exact |
| EEVEE | 8 (of 65,535) | 27,969 | exact |

So Blender writes straight alpha with AgX applied to the unpremultiplied color, and the
engine must premultiply after decoding (section 9's rule).

**Batching equality** (batched: one invocation renders every layer; single: one fresh
process per layer with `outputs` set to that layer):

| Variant | Scenes (layers) | Pixels equal | Files equal |
|---|---|---|---|
| pinned, EEVEE | 6 (23) | all | no (date and render-time text chunks) |
| pinned, Cycles CPU | 4 (13) | all | one of 13 |
| today, EEVEE (8-bit, dither on) | 4 (13) | all | no |
| pinned, stamps off, EEVEE and Cycles CPU | 2 + 1 (12) | all | all |
| pinned, Cycles device `auto` (Metal GPU) | 2 (7) | no: ±1 code on 1 to 114 values per layer | no |

Two batched Cycles GPU runs of the same request differ from each other the same way, so
the GPU result is nondeterministic, not a batching effect. Batching saves Blender start
up: 0.6 to 0.7 s per extra process (5 layers: 1.0 s batched against 3.5 s single).

**Item 38 (orthographic framing).** Per-layer alpha bounding boxes, Blender against the
three.js reference, `orthographic` scene (640 × 360, zoom 1.3):

| Layer | Size ratio (x, y) | Center offset ratio (x, y) |
|---|---|---|
| box | 1.800, 1.778 | 1.779, 1.762 |
| ball | 1.821, 1.785 | 1.741, — |
| ring | 1.790, 1.833 | 1.789, 1.765 |
| cone | 1.824, 1.795 | 1.785, 1.759 |

The ratio is the frame aspect (16 / 9 = 1.778): `ortho_scale = orthoHeight / zoom` with
`orthoHeight = top − bottom = 6`, and Blender's AUTO sensor fit applies it to the wider
dimension. Same in EEVEE (pinned and today's worker) and Cycles (1.773 to 1.815). Setting the camera's `sensor_fit`
to `'VERTICAL'` for orthographic cameras makes `ortho_scale` the height, as native.

**Item 39 (glTF texture orientation).** Quadrant colors of the orientation chart on the
`gltf-webp-avif` card, top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right: three.js red,
green, blue, yellow; Blender blue, yellow, red, green (vertically flipped) in EEVEE,
Cycles, and today's worker. `renderSnapshot` passes glTF UVs (origin top-left, textures
with `flipY = false`) to Blender, whose UV origin is bottom-left. Flipping V
(`v → 1 − v`) for glTF-sourced meshes in the snapshot fixes it.

## Defects found (candidates for appendix A)

1. **`renderSnapshot` throws on KHR_materials_unlit models.** It reads `color`,
   `emissive`, `roughness`, `metalness`, and `emissiveIntensity` from every source
   material as a `MeshStandardMaterial`; GLTFLoader's `MeshBasicMaterial` has no
   `emissive`, so `getHexString` of undefined throws and Blender rendering fails for the
   whole composition (`rendering.ts`, the `useSourceMaterials` branch). Reproduced with
   the corpus's extension grid.
2. **Blender snapshots drop EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing instances:** `getVertexPosition` and
   `matrixWorld` ignore instance matrices, so six instanced boxes render as one box at
   the node transform (Blender render of `gltf-pbr-ext`; the geometry check above shows
   no instance matching).
3. **Blender snapshots skip glTF points and lines** (`traverse` keeps `isMesh` only).
4. **Alpha-mask (MASK) glTF materials render opaque in Blender** (observed on the cutout
   card; the snapshot carries `opacity` but no alpha test).
5. **Production picks use a stale skinned bounding sphere.** three.js's
   `SkinnedMesh.raycast` computes the sphere on the first call and keeps it; after the
   pose moves, rays to the moved geometry miss (9 of 6,480 rays in `gltf-skinned`). The
   engine picks against the current pose. Same class as item 40.
6. **Point and line picks use a 1-world-unit threshold** (three.js defaults) divided by
   the object's local scale, so lines and point clouds in models catch rays far from
   them: on the extension grid, 459 of 820 hits (56%) go to the one line cell and the one
   point cell of 20. Parity keeps it; it is a UX defect.
7. **Blender surfaces are not byte-stable:** PNG text chunks carry date and render time.
   Turning stamps off (`render.use_stamp_*`) makes EEVEE and Cycles CPU outputs
   byte-identical.
8. **Cycles on the Metal GPU is nondeterministic** at ±1 of 65,535 (Cycles device `auto`,
   appendix A item 33's machine dependence also applies run to run).

## Recommendations

**Decision 14 (native port of the three.js r180 subset): keep it**, implemented as a port
of three.js's program *generation*, not of its shading by hand:

- Vendor r180's `ShaderChunk`/`ShaderLib` GLSL (MIT) and port `WebGLProgram`'s prefix
  and define assembly to Rust; verify byte-equality against the programs this spike
  records (49 in the corpus; extend the corpus as materials are added). Compile through
  the S3 naga pipeline with the nine fixes above, and cache compiled programs on disk.
- Port `WebGLRenderer`'s pass structure into the 3D scene pass: RGBA-packed shadow
  maps with r180's PCF code, the transmission pass (half-float MSAA target, mip chain,
  only the layer's own objects), the shared depth prepass per depth set, MSAA 4×,
  back-then-front transparency, and three.js's uniform values. The replayer's GL
  emulation is the executable specification of what these produce.
- `pm-render` must write an all-ones sample mask in every 3D fragment shader and compile
  3D shaders with Metal's safe math mode: add the option to wgpu upstream (preferred) or
  carry the wgpu-hal patch in a pinned fork lane.
- Use hand-written WGSL only where profiling demands it; the spike shows it can match
  bit for bit, at the cost of re-deriving every chunk combination.
- `pm-geom3d`: adopt the lazy skinned pick (bounded by per-joint rest boxes), three.js's
  raycast semantics as listed, and per-frame bounds; decode AVIF natively and leave it
  to the browser in the wasm build.

No feature needs the fallback (a three.js pixel source).

**Contract 11 (Blender job protocol, snapshot, surface format):**

- Surface: 16-bit RGBA PNG, straight alpha, AgX codes (E); the engine decodes exactly,
  ignores `gAMA`, `sRGB`, and `iCCP`, and premultiplies in E at f32. Worker settings as
  section 17 plus metadata stamps off.
- Batching a frame's layers in one invocation is allowed: equal pixels for EEVEE and
  Cycles on the CPU. Cycles on the GPU is nondeterministic regardless of batching, so the
  resolved device is recorded per job (the manifest already reports it) and surfaces are
  cached by content key and never re-rendered within a job, as section 17 says.
- Snapshot: orthographic cameras carry an explicit sensor fit (item 38) and glTF-sourced
  meshes an explicit UV convention (item 39). Whether existing projects keep today's
  framing and orientation is Jude's decision (items 38, 39); the spike recommends fixing
  both for new content (`compat` level 2) and keeping level 1 as today. The snapshot
  should also carry instances, points and lines, unlit materials, and alpha mode
  (defects 1 to 4), each at level 2.
- `pm-blender` snapshots built from `pm-geom3d` geometry meet the 1e-6 bound (3.1e-7
  measured with f32 skinning).

## Gaps

- The wgpu side replays the draws three.js issued; it does not generate them. Program
  generation and the pass structure are the Phase 3a port described above.
- Metal safe math needs a wgpu change; without it 79 of 80 outputs pass.
- Corpus gaps listed under "Corpus"; base machines not measured (decision 11); GPU
  timing of the 3D pass and the 3D orbit gate (Phase 3c) not measured.
